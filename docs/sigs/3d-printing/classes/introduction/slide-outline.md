# Slide Outline: Introduction to 3D Printing

## Slide 1: Introduction to 3D Printing

- What 3D printing is.
- What it can make.
- How the basic workflow works.
- When to use a service and when to own a printer.

## Slide 2: What Is 3D Printing?

- A way to make physical objects from digital 3D models.
- Most beginner printers build objects one layer at a time.
- The process is additive: material is added instead of cut away.

## Slide 3: The Basic Idea

- Digital model.
- Slicer.
- Printer instructions.
- Physical object.

## Slide 4: Functional Models

- Replacement parts.
- Brackets, clips, adapters, and mounts.
- Tools, jigs, and fixtures.
- Prototypes to test fit and function.

## Slide 5: Decorative Models

- Figurines and display pieces.
- Ornaments and gifts.
- Props and cosplay pieces.
- Educational and architectural models.

## Slide 6: Where Models Come From

- Downloaded STL files.
- Models designed in CAD software.
- 3D scans.
- Models made or modified by someone else.

## Slide 7: Process Overview

1. Get a 3D model, often an STL.
2. Load it into OrcaSlicer.
3. Choose print settings.
4. Generate G-code.
5. Transfer the job to the printer.
6. Print the model.

## Slide 8: What the Slicer Does

- Places and orients the model.
- Divides the model into layers.
- Adds supports when needed.
- Sets infill and wall thickness.
- Creates G-code for the printer.

## Slide 9: What Is G-code?

- Instructions for the printer.
- Controls motion, temperature, fans, and material flow.
- Tells the printer how to build the object layer by layer.

## Slide 10: Transfer to the Printer

- Wi-Fi.
- USB.
- SD card or other removable media.
- The exact method depends on the printer.

## Slide 11: Printing the Model

- Printer heats the bed and nozzle or prepares its print process.
- The first layer is critical.
- The printer builds the object one layer at a time.
- Finished prints may require cleanup or support removal.

## Slide 12: Why You Might Not Want a Printer

- Specialty printers can be expensive.
- Metal printing may require industrial equipment and fire controls.
- Resin and some processes involve chemicals.
- Some materials produce fumes or particles.
- No single printer prints every material.

## Slide 13: Why You Might Want a Printer

- Frequent printing.
- Fast turnaround.
- Low-cost prototypes.
- Quick design revisions.
- Validate a model before ordering expensive specialty prints.

## Slide 14: Services and Specialty Materials

- Print services can produce parts in materials home printers cannot handle.
- Examples: tool steel, titanium, gold, ceramic, and industrial plastics.
- Home plastic prints can confirm shape, fit, and function first.

## Slide 15: Beginner Advice

- Start simple.
- PLA on an FDM printer is a common beginner path.
- Expect failed prints while learning.
- Preview the sliced model before printing.
- Use services when the material or process is not home-friendly.

## Slide 16: Questions

- What would you want to make?
- Would a service or personal printer fit that need?
- What material would the object need?
