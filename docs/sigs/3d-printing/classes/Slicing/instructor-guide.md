# Instructor Guide: Slicing

## Audience

Beginners who have seen the 3D printing workflow and are ready to understand what happens inside the slicer.

## Key Points

- The slicer converts the model into layers and printer movements.
- Profiles prevent many beginner mistakes by matching the printer and material.
- Orientation can affect strength, surface quality, support need, and print time.
- Preview mode is where many problems can be caught before printing.
- More infill does not automatically mean a better part; walls often matter more.

## Demo Flow

1. Open OrcaSlicer.
2. Select the printer, filament, and process profile.
3. Import a simple model.
4. Show move, rotate, scale, and lay-flat tools.
5. Compare layer heights.
6. Compare low and moderate infill.
7. Turn supports on and off for an overhang example.
8. Slice and inspect preview by layer.
9. Show time and filament estimates.

## Common Questions

Should I use default settings?
: Yes at first. Change one setting at a time after you understand the result.

What infill should I use?
: Many decorative prints use low infill. Functional parts may need more walls, better orientation, or different material.

Why does preview matter?
: Preview shows supports, gaps, thin walls, travel moves, and likely problem areas before material is wasted.
