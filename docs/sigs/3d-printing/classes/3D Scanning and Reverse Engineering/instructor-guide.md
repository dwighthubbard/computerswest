# Instructor Guide: 3D Scanning and Reverse Engineering

## Audience

Attendees who want to duplicate or adapt real-world objects.

## Key Points

- Scanning captures shape, but not design intent.
- Decorative objects are usually better scanning candidates than precise mechanical parts.
- Shiny, transparent, dark, thin, or featureless objects are hard to scan.
- Mesh cleanup is often required before slicing.
- Functional reverse engineering usually combines measurement, sketching, and CAD.
- Copyright and ownership can matter when copying objects.

## Demo Flow

1. Explain scanning versus reverse engineering.
2. Show examples of good and bad scan candidates.
3. Demonstrate a scan viewer or short phone-scanning example if available.
4. Show mesh problems: holes, rough surfaces, extra background geometry.
5. Measure a simple object with calipers.
6. Explain when to model from measurements instead of scanning.

## Common Questions

Can I scan a broken part and print a new one?
: Sometimes, but exact functional parts usually need measurements and CAD cleanup.

Can a phone scan make printable files?
: Sometimes for decorative shapes, but quality varies and cleanup may be needed.

Why does the scan look rough?
: Lighting, surface finish, camera movement, software, and object shape all affect results.
